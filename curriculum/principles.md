# Principles

My own notes, in my own words. Not quotes — if I could not restate an idea
without the book open, it did not belong here.

Format is load-bearing: `scripts/compose.py` parses `## <ID> · <title>` and the
metadata comment under it, then resurfaces one per day, preferring whatever I
have not seen in `principles.cooldown_days` (see `config/settings.yaml`).
Add your own freely — just keep the heading shape and give each a unique ID.

---

## P01 · Enough is a number I pick, or the market picks it for me
<!-- source: The Psychology of Money | tags: greed, comparison, contentment -->

The dangerous part of having no defined "enough" is that the target moves every
time someone near me does better. That is a race with no finish line, and the
people who blow up are rarely the ones who earned too little — they are the ones
who never decided when to stop reaching.

**Prompt:** What monthly number would make me stop checking what other engineers are earning?

## P02 · Getting rich and staying rich are different skills
<!-- source: The Psychology of Money | tags: risk, survival, compounding -->

Getting money rewards optimism and swinging. Keeping it rewards paranoia and
refusing to be forced to sell. Nothing compounds if I get wiped out halfway, so
survival beats returns — the goal is to stay in the game long enough for time to
do the work.

**Prompt:** What could end this income stream entirely, and what would surviving it cost me?

## P03 · The tail carries the whole result
<!-- source: The Psychology of Money | tags: variance, patience, portfolio -->

Most of what I try will do close to nothing. A tiny number of things will carry
everything. That is not failure, that is the shape of the distribution — so the
real error is quitting the portfolio before a tail shows up, not picking wrong.

**Prompt:** Am I judging this stream by its own odds, or by how it compares to a tail I already know about?

## P04 · Controlling my time is the highest dividend money pays
<!-- source: The Psychology of Money | tags: freedom, motivation, why -->

The thing I actually want is not a bigger number, it is waking up and deciding
what the day is for. Worth holding onto, because it reframes a side stream: an
extra ¥50,000/month that costs me every evening may be a worse trade than half
that earned in four hours.

**Prompt:** Does this stream buy back time eventually, or does it just rent more of mine?

## P05 · Automate the behaviour, do not rely on the willpower
<!-- source: I Will Teach You to Be Rich | tags: systems, automation, discipline -->

Every system that depends on me being disciplined on a Tuesday night eventually
fails. The fix is to move the decision upstream — make the transfer, the
publish, the invoice happen on a schedule so that doing nothing is the correct
behaviour.

**Prompt:** What part of this week's plan still depends on me feeling motivated?

## P06 · Spend hard on the few things I love, cut the rest without mercy
<!-- source: I Will Teach You to Be Rich | tags: spending, values, tradeoffs -->

Frugality as a blanket rule makes me miserable and does not move the number
much. Picking two or three things worth real money and being ruthless about
everything else does both jobs at once.

**Prompt:** What did I pay for this month that I would not miss at all?

## P07 · A big win beats a hundred small optimisations
<!-- source: I Will Teach You to Be Rich | tags: leverage, focus, income -->

Cancelling subscriptions is a rounding error next to raising my rate or landing
one client. Both feel like "working on money", but only one changes the slope.
My scattered-focus pattern pulls me toward the small stuff because it is easier
to finish.

**Prompt:** Is today's action a big-win action, or an optimisation I chose because it felt finishable?

## P08 · Make it obvious, easy, and immediately satisfying
<!-- source: Atomic Habits | tags: habits, present-bias, design -->

I have a present bias, so a habit that pays off in six months will not survive.
The counter is to engineer the payoff forward: shrink the task until starting is
trivial, put the cue in my way, and give the win a visible mark the same day.

**Prompt:** What is the smallest version of today's task that still counts as done?

## P09 · Never miss twice
<!-- source: Atomic Habits | tags: habits, recovery, streaks -->

One missed day is noise. Two is the beginning of the new pattern. The rule that
actually protects a streak is not perfection, it is making the very next rep
non-negotiable after a miss.

**Prompt:** If I missed yesterday, what is the two-minute rep that gets me back today?

## P10 · Systems beat goals because goals end
<!-- source: Atomic Habits | tags: systems, identity, consistency -->

A goal is a single event; the system is what happens every week regardless. When
I hit a goal the behaviour stops, and when I miss it I feel like a failure. The
weekly metric matters more than the 90-day target.

**Prompt:** What is the repeatable weekly motion here, independent of whether I hit the number?

## P11 · I become the kind of person the evidence says I am
<!-- source: Atomic Habits | tags: identity, evidence, consistency -->

Each time I ship something small, I cast a vote for "I am someone who ships".
Ten votes beat one dramatic effort, which is exactly why the daily action exists
at all — it is not about the task, it is about the accumulating evidence.

**Prompt:** What did I do this week that a person who finishes things would have done?

## P12 · Pay myself first, before anything else touches the money
<!-- source: The Richest Man in Babylon | tags: saving, automation, discipline -->

A fixed slice comes out the moment money arrives, not from whatever survives the
month. Whatever remains expands to fill the space available, every time, so the
order of operations is the whole trick.

**Prompt:** When the first yen from this stream lands, where does it go before I can think about it?

## P13 · Take advice only from people who have done the thing
<!-- source: The Richest Man in Babylon | tags: advice, judgment, sources -->

Advice about earning from someone who has not earned that way is entertainment.
The filter is simple — has this person actually done it, recently, at roughly my
scale? Applies to half of what I read online.

**Prompt:** Has the person behind today's stream signal actually made money this way?

## P14 · Put the gold to work, do not just pile it up
<!-- source: The Richest Man in Babylon | tags: compounding, assets, income -->

Money sitting still is a worse version of itself every year. The point of saving
is to buy something that earns — which is also the argument for climbing the
ladder from selling hours toward owning assets.

**Prompt:** Does this rung buy me an asset, or am I still renting out hours?

## P15 · Ask about their life, not about my idea
<!-- source: The Mom Test | tags: validation, demand, interviews -->

The moment I describe the idea, everyone gets polite and the data is gone. The
questions that survive contact are about what they did last time, what it cost
them, and what they already tried — facts about the past, not opinions about my
future.

**Prompt:** What question could I ask that would be useful even if my idea is wrong?

## P16 · Compliments are not evidence, money and time are
<!-- source: The Mom Test | tags: validation, demand, signals -->

"That sounds great" costs nothing to say. A signed invoice, a calendar slot, or
an intro to their boss costs something real. Only the second kind counts as
proof of demand, and the rubric weights it that way on purpose.

**Prompt:** What did the person actually give up — money, time, or a reputation risk?

## P17 · Dig into the problem people already pay to avoid
<!-- source: The Mom Test | tags: demand, pricing, problems -->

If someone has already cobbled together a spreadsheet, hired a freelancer, or
bought a mediocre tool, the budget exists and the pain is proven. That is a far
better starting point than a problem nobody has bothered to work around.

**Prompt:** What ugly workaround are people using right now instead of this?

## P18 · Commit to the next step, or it was just a nice chat
<!-- source: The Mom Test | tags: validation, sales, momentum -->

Every conversation should end with them giving up something — a next meeting, an
intro, a deposit. If there is no ask, I have collected sentiment and called it
research.

**Prompt:** What is the specific ask at the end of my next conversation?

## P19 · Wealth is what I did not spend, not what I earned
<!-- source: The Millionaire Next Door | tags: wealth, spending, signals -->

Income is the flow and wealth is the accumulated stock, and they correlate far
less than I assume. Most of the visibly rich are financing a look; most of the
actually wealthy are boring about it.

**Prompt:** Would this month look different if I measured what I kept instead of what I made?

## P20 · Offence and defence both have to be played
<!-- source: The Millionaire Next Door | tags: spending, earning, balance -->

Earning more (offence) without controlling outflow (defence) leaves me exactly
where I started, just busier. Defence is the less interesting half and therefore
the one I skip.

**Prompt:** Which half am I avoiding this month, and why is it the boring one?

## P21 · Build the thing that earns whether or not I show up
<!-- source: The Millionaire Next Door | tags: assets, leverage, ladder -->

The people who get there mostly own something — equity, a practice, a product —
rather than trade hours at a high rate. Selling hours is the fastest first rung
and the worst last one.

**Prompt:** What would have to be true for this stream to earn during a week I take off?
