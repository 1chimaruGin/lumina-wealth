# The planning fallacy applied to wealth

The planning fallacy: you estimate tasks take less time than they actually do. Applied to wealth, you think five years gets you to serious money. The outside view—people who actually accumulated it—shows something different.

Here's the mechanism. A Tokyo engineer earning 6 million yen annually, saving 2 million per year, reaches 10 million in five years with zero returns. But 10 million invested doesn't double every five years. If real returns (adjusted for inflation and taxes) average 2–3% annually, as Japanese equity returns have roughly shown, then 10 million becomes approximately 11.3 million in five more years. You planned five years; it's now ten, and you've reached only 11.3 million.

Push further: reaching 50 million takes roughly 20–25 years at this savings rate and return. The calculation is math, not optimism. Yet most individual plans assume it happens in 7–10 years.

The outside-view correction comes from tracking actual wealth builders. People who accumulated significant capital reported their actual timelines were 1.5 to 3 times longer than initial plans. This is consistent across professions and countries. Wealth accumulation surveys show the same pattern: substantial sums take decades, not years.

What waiting costs: inflation. A yen earned and invested today beats one earned and invested five years from now, because prices have risen. The longer your timeline stretches, the more inflation erodes your target. Delay three years, and you've forfeited compound growth on three years of savings, plus you're now saving toward a larger nominal number.

What it buys: realistic strategy choices now. Do you increase income faster? Accept a longer timeline? Stop pursuing this and invest time in other returns? Most people avoid this decision and drift instead, wondering why they're behind.

**Key idea.** Most people plan for five years when outside-view data shows twenty is typical. This gap is the real planning fallacy.

**Hard truth.** Your timeline is 20+ years at aggressive savings rates. Inflation reduces this value constantly. People who succeeded either earned significantly more or accepted decades-long horizons. Most shortcuts failed.

**Check it yourself:** Calculate how much you'd accumulate by age 45: (annual savings after expenses) × 20 years, plus 2.5% annual compounding. Does this match your wealth goal? If not, the timeline is longer.
