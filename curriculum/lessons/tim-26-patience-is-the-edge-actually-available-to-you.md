# Patience is the edge actually available to you

Holding a position for 20 years costs roughly 1/20th the taxes and fees of rebalancing monthly. On a ¥100,000 position, a single trade costs roughly ¥99 on major Japanese brokers (Rakuten, IB). Rebalance 12 times per year and you're paying ~1.2% annually in trading fees alone. For capital gains on Japanese-listed equities, add 20.315% tax on profits (verify current rates with NTA, as they change; other instruments like crypto are taxed differently).

Vanguard research showed that the average investor lags the market by 1–2% annually—not because markets are rigged, but because investors trade too much, buying high and selling low, while paying transaction costs and taxes. Over 20 years, a 2% annual drag compounds to roughly 30–40% total underperformance. Holding eliminates both fees and most tax events.

The edge is clear and old: compound growth erases timing mistakes. Hold for 20 years and you benefit from returns on returns, compounding invisibly. Trade monthly and every trade is a tax event and a fee event, chipping away at your growth.

But here is the hard part: this edge only compounds money you already own. You have zero capital. That makes patience theoretical for you right now. The edge is future-facing.

The cost of delaying deployment (once you have capital) is real: ¥100,000 invested at roughly 7% annual returns (historical average for global equities, not guaranteed) becomes ~¥385,000 over 20 years. Delay one year and you lose roughly ¥25,000 in final value. But you cannot incur that cost until you have capital to delay.

Your current edge is discipline, not patience. Build the habit of holding now, in theory, so when capital arrives you already know how to sit still through downturns. Most retail investors crack and trade. If you train yourself to be different before real money is on the line, you'll have an edge.

**Key idea.** Holding beats trading; every trade is a tax event and a fee event. This edge only compounds capital you already own.

**Hard truth.** Patience only compounds capital you deploy. With zero capital, this edge is deferred. Literature on long-term investing assumes you have money to hold—you don't yet. Years learning about this now are sunk cost unless they build psychological readiness for later.

**Check it yourself:** Pick your intended broker and position size. Calculate annual fee drag: (per-trade fee) × (12 monthly trades) ÷ (position size) = annual drag %. For ¥99 fee on ¥50,000 position, that's 2.4% drag—before you earn any returns.
