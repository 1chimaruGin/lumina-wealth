# Boredom as a signal

The average investor underperforms the broad market by approximately 3-4 percentage points annually. Dalbar Inc., which has tracked this since 1984, found the gap comes almost entirely from trading too much and at the wrong times. When markets rise, people buy in. When they fall, they sell. The mechanical cost is significant: a 7% annual return can become 4-5% after you trade 12 times instead of once. Transaction costs (brokerage, spread, slippage) typically run 0.1-0.3% per trade on listed Japanese equities; multiply by frequent buying and selling and you erode 1-2 percentage points just in fees.

The psychological mechanism is simpler. A boring strategy—buy a low-cost index, hold it, rebalance once a year—feels like nothing is working because you see no movement, no wins, no active validation. A strategy that trades daily or weekly feels like it *is* working because you see feedback every session. That feedback is almost always distraction. Markets move noise at high frequency and signal at low frequency. The more frequently you check, the more noise drowns the signal, and the more you tinker.

What makes it worse: boredom is a signal of correctness, not failure. Researchers including Odean and Barber have shown that individual investors who trade most actively consistently underperform those who trade least. You can verify this in academic literature on investor behavior.

For you without capital to deploy, this is perverse luck. You cannot afford to trade frequently. That constraint, which feels like a limitation, is actually your structural advantage if you have the discipline to see it that way. Most people with capital cannot tolerate watching it sit. You're forced to. The danger is that the psychological weight—doing nothing while others appear busy—will eventually crack you. That's the real cost of boring. It is cheap in money; expensive in willpower.

**Key idea.** Frequent trading erodes returns; boredom signals a working strategy, but most quit from impatience before gains compound.

**Hard truth.** Without capital, forced inactivity is your only real advantage. The cost: psychological—watching others trade while you wait. Most people cannot endure this and abandon. The game has one rule: you win if you stay still.

**Check it yourself:** Check SBI or Rakuten's current fee table for listed equity trades. Note the percentage cost per trade, then calculate the annual cost impact on a ¥500,000 portfolio with 10 trades per year versus 1 trade per year.
