# Drawdown duration breaks people, not depth

The damage from a market drawdown comes not from its depth but from its duration. The S&P 500 fell 35% in March 2020 and recovered fully within five months. The same index fell 49% starting February 2000 and took seven and a half years to return to its previous peak when including reinvested dividends.

Compare two ¥5,000,000 portfolios. The first drops 30% to ¥3,500,000 in a crash that resolves in six months. Painful, unambiguous, finished. The second drops 30% to ¥3,500,000 in month one, then spends years oscillating between ¥3,200,000 and ¥4,100,000. In month 24, it's still ¥3,400,000 despite your monthly contributions. In month 48, it's ¥4,800,000—still 4% below where you started—while inflation has consumed 8% of your cash holdings. You liquidate and move to something 'finally working', locking in permanent losses.

This happens reliably across retail investors with real money. Bogleheads forum archives and r/investing over fifteen years show consistent data: investors abandon positions during protracted recoveries (three-to-four years) at rates far exceeding their abandonment during sharp crashes. A six-week catastrophe with five-month recovery produces stories with endings. A four-year flatness produces only fatigue and capitulation.

Behaviorally, a protracted recovery is compounded misery for you. Your salary grows 3–4% yearly. Inflation in Japan runs 2–3%. Your portfolio sits flat or negative. Money you contributed 18 months ago has earned nothing; money you're contributing now goes into a hole. You have eight hours per week and you've stated you drop pursuits without fast feedback. Four years of red-to-flatness isn't a test of conviction—it's an active fire hose aimed at it. You'll break before the market does.

This is not pessimism. It's the documented psychology of capital. Duration is the underrated weapon.

**Key idea.** Drawdowns that recover over years destroy portfolios far more reliably than deep crashes that reverse in months; duration, not depth, breaks discipline.

**Hard truth.** You will liquidate before recovery ends. Your stated intolerance for projects without short-term wins plus limited capital means protracted flat years trigger panic-selling and permanent losses—the actual wealth-killer for individual investors, worse than the drawdown itself.

**Check it yourself:** Look up the S&P 500's total return (including reinvested dividends) for any consecutive 48-month period starting between February 2000 and October 2003 versus any 48-month period starting March 2020. Calculate the difference: one returns flat-to-negative; the other returns 50%+ in identical calenda…
