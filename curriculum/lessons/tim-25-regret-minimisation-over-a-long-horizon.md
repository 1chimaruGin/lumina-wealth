# Regret minimisation over a long horizon

Bezos's regret-minimisation framework is simple: imagine yourself at 80, looking back. Which choice would you regret more—attempting something ambitious and failing, or never trying at all? Pick the path that minimises retrospective regret.

The mechanism is narrative, not optimisation. You construct a backwards identity story: "I'm someone who took the shot." When a venture fails, regret minimisation lets you reframe it: "I'm someone who had the courage to fail." It's emotionally satisfying and hard to argue against, because you're not predicting outcomes. You're predicting how you'll feel about your choices at 80.

Here's what it actually optimises: the coherence and flattery of the narrative you'll tell later. Not expected value. Not wealth. Not even happiness—regret is one emotion among dozens you'll experience over 50 years.

The critical asymmetry: it assumes you can afford to fail. Bezos had approximately $250,000 from his parents and retained income to cushion Amazon's early years. When your existing wealth caps your downside, taking a 30% chance of catastrophic loss is survivable. Without capital, it's not.

For someone with no spare capital and eight hours a week, opportunity cost is brutal. That framework doesn't account for what you're not doing while a low-probability project runs. You're not accruing credentials. You're not building a track record that helps you hire people later or raise capital. You're not learning something with immediate labour-market value.

Regret minimisation lets you spend two years on a project with a 5% success rate, then tell yourself later the story justified it. Expected value asks: what did that cost you? For people like you, the answer is substantial.

**Key idea.** Regret minimisation optimises for the story you'll tell at 80, not for expected value or opportunity cost.

**Hard truth.** This works if you have capital to burn. You don't. Two years chasing a 5% bet costs you credentials, income, and career momentum you won't recover. The framework mostly amplifies capital that already exists.

**Check it yourself:** Calculate your real opportunity cost: 8 hours/week × 52 weeks × your hourly rate in yen. Compare against what any actual project generates. If you've tracked it before, pull a real number from a statement, not an estimate.
