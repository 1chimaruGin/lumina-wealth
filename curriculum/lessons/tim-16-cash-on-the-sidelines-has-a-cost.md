# Cash on the sidelines has a cost

When you hold cash waiting for a market correction or a better entry price, you're holding a position that typically returns less than the alternative. Here's the math: Japanese bank accounts currently earn 0.02% to 0.1% annually, depending on the bank. The Nikkei 225 has returned approximately 6-7% annually over the long term (though with significant volatility). The opportunity cost of waiting is roughly that 6-7% spread per year.

Suppose you have 500,000 JPY you plan to invest, but you're waiting for a 15% pullback that "should be coming soon." Five years pass without a crash. The market averages 5% per year. Your cash has now cost you approximately 140,000 JPY in foregone gains. The pullback comes for two weeks and recovers before you act. Either way, you've paid the cost.

This sharpens in real time. A 5% annual return is roughly 0.4% per month. Waiting one month for a "better setup" costs you, in expectation, 0.4% of your capital. Waiting six months costs about 2.4%. Over years, the bill compounds.

The mechanism is straightforward. Cash is safe but nearly worthless—it doesn't earn. Equities are volatile but have historically paid a premium. Waiting trades one risk (drawdowns) for another (missing gains). Most people underestimate the second because losses are visible and opportunity costs are invisible.

There is one conditional: if you genuinely have evidence (not hope) that a crash is imminent, waiting can win. But market timing requires skill most people don't have. The base rate is poor. Studies show most traders underperform a simple buy-and-hold strategy by 1-3% annually due to timing decisions. Waiting for a "perfect entry" usually means entering much later, or not at all.

**Key idea.** Holding cash to time the market costs you the historical equity premium, typically 6-7% yearly, whether you win the timing bet or not.

**Hard truth.** You lack capital now, so this applies to your future. But the hard part: waiting for perfect entry costs compound returns. Timing the market is difficult; studies show most traders underperform buy-and-hold by 1-3% annually. Delayed entry usually costs more than the pullback would gain.

**Check it yourself:** Find your bank's current savings rate (MUFG, Rakuten, SBI). Calculate one year of growth on 100,000 JPY. Compare to the Nikkei 225 return over the same year. The difference is what waiting cost you.
