# Timing and patience are opposites

Timing and patience describe opposite positions, but most investors conflate them into a single false activity: "wait for the right moment, then hold." That's neither.

Timing is the prediction that you can identify when prices will move and act on that prediction. Patience is the decision to hold regardless of price and wait for the underlying business to compound. They are mutually exclusive.

The evidence on timing is direct. Retail investors who trade actively underperform buy-and-hold index returns by 3-4% annually, after fees and taxes. The S&P 500 illustrates the cost of trying: over the past 30 years, missing just the 10 best days cut annualized returns from approximately 10% to 4%. Those 10 days were unannounced and unpredictable in advance.

The mechanism destroying returns is multi-part. Each trade costs transaction fees: a typical buy or sell in Japan incurs 0.10-0.20% in costs. Four trades per year eats 0.4-0.8% of performance before tax. In Japan, capital gains on listed securities incur 20.315% flat tax (源泉徴収 on 上場株式); confirm against current NTA guidance as rates change. The compounding effect of paying tax less frequently is substantial. Worse, the act of constant monitoring triggers poor decisions: research in behavioral finance documents that investors who check portfolios daily make worse decisions than those checking quarterly. They sell low after panic and buy high after euphoria.

Patience produces the opposite outcome. It requires holding through losses, price declines, bear markets. There is no daily feedback. There is no visible progress for months. But if the underlying company or index increases in real value, compounding works alone.

You cannot optimise for both. Activity-seeking leads to timing. Patience leads to returns. The conflict is not intellectual—it is behavioral.

**Key idea.** Timing predicts price moves; patience ignores them. Most who try timing destroy returns via fees, taxes, and decisions driven by daily price changes.

**Hard truth.** Patience produces no daily wins—months pass with no feedback. Timing destroys 3-4% annually through fees, taxes, and poor decisions. You cannot optimize for both. The pattern you choose now, without capital, will dictate your results once capital arrives.

**Check it yourself:** Calculate: (brokerage buy-sell fee × 4 trades per year) ÷ investment amount × 100 = annual % cost. This is the tax-free cost of timing, before any wrong calls.
