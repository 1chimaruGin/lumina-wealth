# Revenue-based financing

Revenue-based financing (RBF) is a way to get capital without debt or equity. A lender provides money upfront and you repay it as a percentage of your monthly revenue until they've recovered their principal plus a return. Platforms like Clearco and Pipe offer this.

The key difference from debt: you have no fixed monthly payment. If you earn ¥500,000 in a month, you might pay 5% (¥25,000); if you earn ¥50,000, you pay ¥2,500. This protects you during slow periods. The key difference from equity: the funder doesn't own part of your company. You keep full control and all future profits after repayment.

RBF beats debt when your revenue is unpredictable. A lender might want ¥30,000 monthly regardless of whether you made ¥80,000 or ¥20,000 that month. RBF aligns repayment with actual cashflow. It beats equity when you're confident your business will work. With equity, an investor owns part of your company forever; with RBF, you owe a percentage of revenue for a limited time. Once you've repaid, all future revenue is yours alone.

RBF works best for subscription businesses or e-commerce with predictable, recurring revenue. It doesn't work for one-off consulting or lumpy projects—lenders need to forecast what you'll repay reliably.

The catch: RBF lenders want proof of concept first. They typically require months of revenue history and unit economics that already work. You can't walk in with an idea; you need paying customers. Understanding RBF now helps you plan: once your side project has revenue, you have a third option beyond 'bootstrap forever' or 'dilute your equity.'

**Key idea.** Trade a percentage of future revenue for capital now, keep your equity, repay only when you earn.
