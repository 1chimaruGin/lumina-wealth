# Base rates for how long things take

Base rates are the actual frequencies something happens. A small business takes an average of 18–36 months to first profit, assuming it survives; 70% fail before year five. A technical skill requires roughly 10,000 hours to genuine competence—at 8 hours weekly, that's roughly 25 years of consistent work. A diversified equity portfolio compounds at roughly 5–7% annually in real terms; Japan's Nikkei 225 averaged around 4% nominal over three decades. Zero capital means compounding never starts.

The mechanism: compounding requires both capital and time. At 6% real annual return, capital doubles every 12 years. At zero starting capital, it stays zero. A business requires months or years of zero income while you guess what customers want; when the guess is wrong—the typical case—you burn time and restart. Skill acquisition is nonlinear: the first 1,000 hours produce almost nothing, the next 1,000 build momentum, the next 1,000 approach competence.

Your constraints: 8 hours weekly and zero capital, combined with a pattern of dropping projects. Income streams usually die where timeline meets boredom—6–18 months in, before the idea proves itself, before grind becomes competence. Most bootstrapped projects signal whether they work within 18–36 months. An engineer switching technical specializations needs 2–3 years at 40+ weekly hours to hire at market rate in the new skill. At 8 hours weekly, add 3–5 more years.

Historical returns: Japan's stock market returned roughly 4% nominal annually over three decades. US equities averaged 10% nominal. In Japan, listed equity gains face flat 20.315% taxation (confirm with current NTA 所得税法); cryptocurrency gains are 雑所得 and taxed progressively—different instruments, completely different tax regimes. Real interest rates in Japan have been near-zero for decades. Any strategy based on market returns requires: capital, years untouched without selling, and acceptance of 70%+ of years where you lose money.

**Key idea.** Most things that look fast on the surface—business profit, skill mastery, wealth accumulation—follow predictable timelines that are longer than people expect.

**Hard truth.** Without capital, compounding does not start. With 8 hours weekly and a history of dropping projects, an income stream needs 2–3 years minimum of grinding dull work before money arrives. Most people quit before the timeline resolves.

**Check it yourself:** Calculate: 10,000 hours ÷ (8 hours weekly × 52 weeks yearly) = your timeline to baseline skill competence at your stated pace. Write down the number.
