# Athens, silver and empire

The Laurion mines in Attica produced roughly 50 tonnes of pure silver per year at peak extraction around the 5th century BCE. When Athens' assembly voted to spend the silver on 200 warships instead of distributing it to citizens, they were voting to convert ore into seapower. At Salamis in 480 BCE, the Persian fleet was decisively defeated by Athenian triremes. The silver mines had created the specific material conditions for that victory—and for Athens' domination of the Mediterranean for the next century.

The owl tetradrachm, minted from this silver, became the dominant currency across the Greek world and beyond. Its stability and purity created a network effect: merchants accepted it everywhere, which meant its acceptance accelerated, which meant more merchants needed it to trade. By the 4th century, it was found in trade routes from Spain to India.

But the mines operated at terrible human cost. Modern estimates place the total workforce at 20,000 to 30,000 slaves at peak production—roughly 5% of Athens' total population in slavery. These were not skilled workers earning wages; they were property, disposable labour. Conditions were brutal: tunnels collapsed, water flooded the deep shafts, and most slaves died in the mines within years.

The power the silver created was entirely dependent on three specific things: continued extraction, military force to defend the mines and enforce trade dominance, and merchants' willingness to accept the coins. As deposits became depleted and harder to work, the supply of new coin diminished. Athens' ability to control trade routes declined. Athens' wealth and power declined with it.

**Key idea.** Monetary dominance flows from control of a scarce resource and military power to enforce its distribution.

**Hard truth.** The money worked because Athens compelled its use through naval force and enslaved tens of thousands to extract it. Depletion inevitable. Without capital, this path is closed—you cannot find silver or deploy navies. The lesson applies only if you control something unique others need.

**Check it yourself:** Look up the current price of silver per tonne. Multiply by 50 to estimate annual Laurion revenue in today's dollars at current market rates. This gives you the scale of wealth the empire ran on.
