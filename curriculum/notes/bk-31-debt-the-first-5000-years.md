# Debt: The First 5000 Years

*David Graeber, 2011*

Graeber argues that credit systems, not barter, were humanity's first economy—debt relationships, not individual exchange, are primary to human societies. Ancient practices like debt jubilees show that perpetual indebtedness is not inevitable but a recent capitalist choice. The standard economic narrative (barter→money→markets) is historically false, and humans are embedded in moral obligations, not natural economic rationalists.

**The one idea.** Debt jubilees and forgiveness were standard in ancient societies; perpetual indebtedness is capitalism's anomaly, not the norm.

**Verdict.** The book reshapes your understanding of money's history with genuine intellectual depth. However: it runs 534 pages—a long commitment for someone with 8 hours/week and your pattern of dropping projects. The core argument lands in chapters 1–4; the rest is historical illustration.
