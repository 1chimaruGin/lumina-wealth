## 2 · Film

### The Big Short
<sub>Adam McKay · 2015 · `time` · film</sub>

The housing crisis wasn't driven by intentional fraud but by structural incentive misalignment: mortgage originators earned commissions without bearing default risk (they sold immediately), rating agencies were paid by issuers they rated, and banks had no skin in loans they packaged. This created a system where everyone's incentives pointed toward ignoring risk. The film shows how being mathematically correct about an unsustainable system provides zero protection if the system can stay irrational longer than your capital endures.

> **The one idea.** Institutional incentive misalignment sustains wrong prices indefinitely. Being right early means insolvency before vindication.

**Verdict.** Worth watching for understanding how institutional incentives drive market mispricing and the psychology of being structurally correct but temporarily insolvent. Better as entertainment than instruction—stylistic flourishes prioritize narrative momentum over mechanical clarity. Watch FOR the incentive failures, not FO…

**Caveat.** Treats fraud as extraordinary and intentional when it was ordinary systemic incentive failure. Being right early is dramatized as heroic vindication; in reality it destroyed investor capital for years. Inverts the core lesson from 'smart people saw it coming' to 'smart people got destroyed first.'
